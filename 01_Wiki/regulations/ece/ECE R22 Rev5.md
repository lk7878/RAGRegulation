# UN Regulation No. 22 (Revision 5)

## 1. Scope
This Regulation applies to protective helmets for drivers and passengers of mopeds and of motor cycles with or without side-car and to the visors fitted to such helmets or intended to be added to them.

## 2. Definitions
Key definitions include:
*   **2.1. "Protective helmet"**: A helmet primarily intended to protect the wearer's head against impact.
*   **2.5. "Retention system"**: The complete assembly by which the helmet is maintained in position on the head.
*   **2.7. "Lower face cover (chin guard)"**: A detachable, movable, or integral part covering the lower face.
*   **2.8. "Visor"**: A transparent protective screen extending over the eyes.
*   **2.17. "Protective helmet type"**: A category of helmets with defined characteristics from the same manufacturer.
*   **2.18. "Visor type"**: A category of visors with similar essential characteristics.

## 3. Application for Approval
Specifies the documentation and samples required for type approval applications for helmets (with or without visors) and for visors separately.

## 4. Markings
Helmets and visors submitted for approval must bear the applicant's trade name/mark, size, year of production, and warnings if applicable (e.g., unsuitability for night use). Markings must be legible, wear-resistant, and not within the main visibility area.

## 5. Approval
*   **5.1. Approval of a protective helmet type**: Describes the approval process, assignment of an approval number (starting with "06" for the current series), and the required international approval mark (a circle surrounding "E", country code, approval number, slash with protection code (J, P, NP, P/J), and serial number).
*   **5.2. Approval of a visor type**: Similar process for visors approved separately.
*   **5.3. Approval of a Sun Shield**: Requirements for sun shields integrated with helmets.

## 6. General Specifications
Comprehensive requirements for helmet construction and performance:
*   **6.1-6.14**: Covers basic construction (hard shell, energy-absorbing padding, retention system), coverage area, external projections, internal sharp edges, assembly, retention system details (chin strap width, fasteners), material durability, and post-test integrity.
*   **6.15. Peripheral vision**: Defines minimum field of vision requirements.
*   **6.16. Visors**: Requirements for attachment, opening angle, field of vision, luminous transmittance (≥80% for clear, ≥35% or ≥20% for photochromic with "DAYTIME USE ONLY" marking), optical defects, light diffusion, color signal recognition, refractive powers, and optional mist-retardant properties.
*   **6.17. Sun shield**: Similar optical requirements as for visors.
*   **6.18. Conspicuity marking**: Optional requirements for reflective materials to enhance user visibility, including geometry, colorimetry, photometry, and durability.

## 7. Tests
Detailed test procedures and acceptance criteria:
*   **7.1-7.2**: Test matrix and conditioning types (ambient, heat, low-temperature, UV/moisture).
*   **7.3. Linear Impact – energy absorption tests**: Primary test method using guided free-fall onto flat or kerbstone anvils. Measures acceleration and Head Injury Criterion (HIC). Limits: ≤275 g and HIC ≤2400 for standard impacts.
*   **7.4. Test for projections and surface friction**: Two methods (A or B) to assess risk of rotational injury from projections or high friction.
*   **7.5. Rigidity tests**: Measures deformation under compressive load.
*   **7.6. Dynamic test of the retention system**: Tests strap strength under dynamic load.
*   **7.7. Retention (detaching) test**: Ensures helmet stays on headform under shock.
*   **7.8. Visor tests**: Includes mechanical impact test, high-speed particle test, and optical tests (transmittance, diffusion, abrasion resistance).
*   **7.9. Sun shield tests**: Optical tests similar to visors.
*   **7.10-7.12**: Tests for chin strap micro-slip, abrasion resistance, and quick-release mechanisms.
*   **7.13. Oblique impact test method of measuring rotational acceleration**: Measures rotational acceleration and Brain Injury Criterion (BrIC). Limits: PRA ≤10,400 rad/s², BrIC ≤0.78.
*   **7.14. Test of photochromic, liquid crystal or equivalent visors**: Specific tests for variable transmittance visors.

## 8. Test Reports
Technical services must prepare and retain test reports for 10 years.

## 9. Production Qualification
Procedures for qualifying a production line before series production begins, including sampling plans and statistical acceptance criteria for helmets and visors.

## 10. Conformity of Production and Routine Tests
Ongoing production control requirements based on batch sampling, with defined sample sizes, tests, and acceptance/rejection criteria for both normal and strengthened control levels.

## 11. Modification and Extension of Approval
Procedures for notifying and approving modifications to an approved helmet or visor type.

## 12. Penalties for Non-conformity of Production
Approval may be withdrawn if production conformity requirements are not met.

## 13. Production Definitively Discontinued
Procedure for notifying authorities when production of an approved type ceases.

## 14. Information for Wearers
Mandatory labeling and information requirements for helmets and visors placed on the market, including warnings about fit, replacement after impact, and cleaning.

## Annexes
The Regulation includes 19 Annexes detailing test methods, communication forms, approval mark examples, headform specifications, and other technical procedures. Key annexes mentioned include:
*   **Annex 1A, 1B**: Communication forms.
*   **Annex 2A, 2B**: Examples of approval marks.
*   **Annex 3**: Diagram of protective helmets.
*   **Annex 4, 5, 6**: Headforms and positioning.
*   **Annex 7**: Oblique impact test.
*   **Annex 8**: Test machines.
*   **Annex 9-11, 13-19**: Various test procedures (visor opening angle, abrasion, optical properties, refractive power, mist-retardant, high-speed particle, photochromic, etc.).
*   **Annex 12**: Type approval scheme (flow chart).
