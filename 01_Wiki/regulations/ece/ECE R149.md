# UN Regulation No. 149

## 1. Scope
This Regulation applies to the following road illumination devices:
*   Headlamps emitting a driving-beam and/or an asymmetrical passing-beam for vehicles of categories L, M, N and T.
*   Adaptive front-lighting systems (AFS) for vehicles of categories M and N.
*   Headlamps emitting a driving-beam and/or a symmetrical passing-beam for vehicles of categories L and T.
*   Front fog lamps for vehicles of categories L3, L4, L5, L7, M, N and T.
*   Cornering lamps for vehicles of categories M, N and T.

## 2. Definitions
For the purpose of this Regulation, the definitions given in the latest series of amendments to UN Regulation No. 48 in force at the time of application for type approval shall apply, unless otherwise specified. Key definitions include "road illumination devices of different types", which are distinguished by trade name/mark, optical system characteristics, inclusion of components altering optical effects, suitability for traffic direction, beam type, AFS functions/modes/classes, light source category/LED module code, etc.

## 3. Administrative Provisions
### 3.1. Application for Approval
The application must be submitted by the holder of the trade name or their representative, accompanied by detailed drawings, technical descriptions, and samples. Specific information is required depending on the device type (e.g., headlamp class, AFS functions, light source details).

### 3.2. Approval
If the submitted devices meet the requirements, approval shall be granted with a unique approval number. Notices of approval, extension, refusal, or withdrawal are communicated to Contracting Parties.

### 3.3. Approval Mark
Devices must bear a space for a Unique Identifier (UI) or, if technically not possible, a conventional approval mark. The approval marking consists of a circle with the letter "E" and the country distinguishing number, the approval number, the Regulation number (R149) followed by the series of amendments, and additional symbols (e.g., arrows for traffic direction, "PL" for plastic lenses, luminous intensity reference mark). Other required markings include the trade name, light source categories, rated voltage/wattage, and specific identification codes for modules.

### 3.4. – 3.8. Modifications, Conformity of Production, Penalties, etc.
Procedures for modification of an approved type, extension of approval, conformity of production (referring to Annexes 2 and 3), penalties for non-conformity, notification of discontinued production, and communication of Technical Services and Approval Authorities are outlined.

## 4. General Technical Requirements
Lamps must be designed to ensure satisfactory operation under normal conditions of use and vibration. They must provide adequate illumination without dazzle (passing-beam) and good illumination (driving-beam). An adjustment device must be fitted unless other means are provided on the vehicle.

### 4.5. Light Sources
*   **Restrictions:** Lamps shall only be equipped with light sources approved under UN Regulations Nos. 37, 99, and/or 128, and/or LED modules, subject to specific restrictions per device class (e.g., Class D headlamps, front fog lamps, AFS).
*   **General Requirements:** Includes provisions for replaceable light sources, electronic control gear, electromagnetic compatibility (for Class D/ES), and marking of high luminous flux (>2000 lm).
*   **Specific Requirements:** Details for cornering lamps with light source modules, and headlamps/AFS/front fog lamps with LED sources/modules, including luminous flux limits for various classes.

### 4.6. Testing of the Lamp
Specifies test conditions (voltage, standard light sources, correction factors) for different light source types: replaceable filament, gas-discharge, LED, non-replaceable, and those with control gear.

### 4.7. – 4.17. Additional Requirements
*   Testing of plastic lenses (Annex 8).
*   Sharpness/linearity of cut-off (Annexes 5, 6).
*   Stability of photometric performance (Annex 7, except cornering lamps).
*   Provisions for lamps adaptable for right/left-hand traffic.
*   Testing of mechanical/electromechanical structures (robustness, failure modes).
*   Illumination configuration for different traffic conditions.
*   Colour of light emitted (white, selective yellow for front fog lamps optional).
*   Requirements for headlamps/AFS with adjustable reflectors.

## 5. Specific Technical Requirements
### 5.1. Driving-Beam (Classes A, B, D, BS, CS, DS, ES)
Specifies aiming procedure and luminous intensity distribution requirements (minimum and maximum values at specific test points) for primary and secondary driving-beams. The maximum luminous intensity (I_M) must not exceed 215,000 cd. A reference mark (I'_M) is calculated and rounded.

### 5.2. Passing-Beam for Headlamps (Classes A, B, D)
Specifies aiming procedure (Annex 5) and detailed luminous intensity requirements at numerous test points and within defined zones (e.g., Zone III, Zone I). Provisions for bend lighting and additional light sources (e.g., for infrared) are included.

### 5.3. Adaptive Front-Lighting Systems (AFS)
#### 5.3.1. General Provisions
Systems must provide a Class C passing-beam and may incorporate additional classes/modes. They must automatically modify beams to ensure good illumination without causing discomfort.

#### 5.3.2. Provisions Concerning the Passing-Beam
Detailed photometric requirements for Class C, V, E, and W passing-beams, including for bending modes (categories 1 and 2). Requirements cover luminous intensities at specific points/zones, cut-off position, and overhead sign illumination.

#### 5.3.3. Provisions Concerning the Driving-Beam
Requirements for the driving-beam in neutral state (meeting Class B requirements) and during adaptation. Table 15 specifies maximum intensity limits for "shaded" areas to avoid glare to oncoming/preceding vehicles and minimum intensity requirements in other areas.

#### 5.3.4. – 5.3.6. Measurement Conditions & Conformity of Production
Describes test procedures for bending modes and outlines specific photometric requirements for conformity of production testing for various AFS modes and classes.
