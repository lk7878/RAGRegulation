**UN Regulation No. 14 - Safety-Belt Anchorages**

This Regulation establishes uniform technical prescriptions for the approval of vehicles with regard to the strength and location of safety-belt anchorages.

**Key Provisions:**

*   **Scope:** Applies to vehicles of categories M and N1.
*   **Definitions:** Detailed definitions for terms such as "vehicle type", "belt anchorages", "effective belt anchorage", "seat", "group of seats", "adjustment system", "reference zone", and "thorax load limiter function".
*   **Approval Process:** Outlines the application procedure, documentation required (drawings, specifications), and the granting of approval with a unique approval number and mark.
*   **Specifications:**
    *   **General:** Anchorages must enable installation of suitable belts, minimize strap slippage and damage, and function in normal use.
    *   **Minimum Number:** Specified in Annex 6, varying by vehicle category (M1, M2, M3, N1, N2, N3) and seating position (front/other, outboard/centre, forward/rearward/side-facing). Requires either 2 anchorages (for lap belts) or 3 anchorages (for three-point belts).
    *   **Location:**
 *   **Lower Anchorages:** Angles (α1, α2) relative to horizontal are specified (typically between 20° and 80°, with specific ranges for M1 vehicles, buckle side vs. non-buckle side, bench seats, etc.). Minimum distance between the two lower anchorages of the same belt is 350 mm (240 mm for certain centre rear seats in M1/N1).
 *   **Upper Anchorages:** Defined geometric zone relative to the occupant's R-point and torso line, ensuring the anchorage is behind, below, and within specified angular limits (planes FN and FK). Minimum distance S from the median plane is 140 mm.
    *   **Dimensions:** Anchorage holes should be threaded 7/16 inch (20 UNF 2B), unless the vehicle is supplied with belts by the manufacturer.
*   **Tests:**
    *   **Static Strength Tests:** Primary method. Specifies general test conditions (vehicle securing, seat position), traction devices (Annex 5), and specific test loads for different belt configurations:
 *   Three-point belt with retractor/pulley (simultaneous loads on upper and lower anchorages).
 *   Three-point belt without retractor.
 *   Lap belt.
 *   Special-type belts.
 *   Tests for rearward-facing and side-facing seats.
 *   Tests for anchorages on seat structures include an additional force representing seat mass.
    *   **Dynamic Test (Alternative):** Described in Annex 7, can be used as an alternative to static tests for groups of seats equipped with 3-point belts with thorax load limiters and where the upper anchorage is on the seat structure. Involves a sled test at 50 km/h deceleration with dummies (Annex 8).
*   **Inspection After Test:** Anchorages must withstand the test load without failure (permanent deformation is acceptable if the load is sustained). Limits are placed on the forward displacement of upper anchorages attached to seat structures. Displacement/locking devices must remain operable.
*   **Conformity of Production:** Requires production vehicles to conform to the approved type, subject to random checks and possible tests.
*   **Transitional Provisions:** Detailed schedule for the application of different series of amendments (06, 07, 08 series).

**Annexes:**
1.  Communication form for approval.
2.  Examples of approval mark arrangements.
3.  Diagrams for location of effective belt anchorages.
4.  Procedure for determining H-point and torso angle (references R.E.3).
5.  Traction device specifications for tests.
6.  Table of minimum number of anchorages per vehicle category and seating position.
7.  Dynamic test as an alternative to static strength test.
8.  Dummy specifications for the dynamic test.
