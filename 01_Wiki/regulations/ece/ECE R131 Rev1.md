# Regulation No. 131
**Uniform provisions concerning the approval of motor vehicles with regard to the Advanced Emergency Braking Systems (AEBS)**

## 1. Scope and purpose
This Regulation applies to the approval of vehicles of category M2, N2, M3 and N3 with regard to an on-board system to avoid or mitigate the severity of a rear-end in lane collision.

## 2. Definitions
- **Advanced Emergency Braking System (AEBS)**: A system which can automatically detect a potential forward collision and activate the vehicle braking system to decelerate the vehicle with the purpose of avoiding or mitigating a collision.
- **Vehicle type with regard to its Advanced Emergency Braking System**: A category of vehicles which do not differ in such essential respects as the manufacturer's trade name or mark; vehicle features which significantly influence the performances of the AEBS; the type and design of the AEBS.
- **Subject vehicle**: The vehicle being tested.
- **Target**: A high volume series production passenger car of category M1 (AA saloon) or, in the case of a soft target, an object representative of such a vehicle in terms of its detection characteristics applicable to the sensor system of the AEBS under test.
- **Moving target**: A target travelling at a constant speed in the same direction and in the centre of the same lane of travel as the subject vehicle.
- **Stationary target**: A target at standstill facing the same direction and positioned on the centre of the same test lane of travel as the subject vehicle.
- **Soft target**: A target that will suffer minimum damage and cause minimum damage to the subject vehicle in the event of a collision.
- **Collision warning phase**: The phase directly preceding the emergency braking phase, during which the AEBS warns the driver of a potential forward collision.
- **Emergency braking phase**: The phase starting when the AEBS emits a braking demand for at least 4 m/s² deceleration to the service braking system of the vehicle.
- **Common space**: An area on which two or more information functions (e.g. symbol) may be displayed, but not simultaneously.
- **Self-check**: An integrated function that checks for a system failure on a semi-continuous basis at least while the system is active.
- **Time to collision (TTC)**: The value of time obtained by dividing the distance between the subject vehicle and the target by the relative speed of the subject vehicle and the target, at an instant in time.

## 3. Application for approval
The application for approval of a vehicle type with regard to the AEBS shall be submitted by the vehicle manufacturer or by his authorized representative, accompanied by required documentation and a representative vehicle.

## 4. Approval
If the vehicle type meets the requirements, approval shall be granted and an approval number assigned. An international approval mark shall be affixed to every conforming vehicle.

## 5. Specifications
### 5.1. General
- Vehicles fitted with an AEBS shall meet the performance requirements and shall be equipped with an anti-lock braking function per Regulation No. 13, Annex 13.
- The effectiveness of the AEBS shall not be adversely affected by magnetic or electrical fields (compliance with Regulation No. 10, 03 series).
- Conformity with the safety aspects of complex electronic control systems shall be shown by meeting the requirements of Annex 4.

### 5.2. Performance requirements
- The system shall provide the driver with appropriate warnings (collision, failure, deactivation).
- Subsequent to the collision warning, there shall be an emergency braking phase to significantly decrease the speed.
- The system shall be active at least within the vehicle speed range of 15 km/h up to the maximum design speed, and at all vehicle load conditions, unless manually deactivated.
- The system shall be designed to minimize false collision warnings and avoid autonomous braking in non-critical situations.

### 5.3. Interruption by the driver
- The AEBS shall provide means for the driver to interrupt the emergency braking phase, and may provide means to interrupt the collision warning phase (especially if haptic warning via braking).
- Interruption may be initiated by any positive action indicating driver awareness.

### 5.4. Deactivation
- If equipped with a manual deactivation means, the AEBS function shall be automatically reinstated at each new ignition cycle.
- A constant optical warning signal shall inform the driver of deactivation.

### 5.5. Warning indication
- Collision warning shall be provided by at least two modes selected from acoustic, haptic, or optical.
- Failure warning shall be a constant yellow optical warning signal.
- Optical warning signals shall be visible by daylight and easily verifiable from the driver's seat.

### 5.6. Provisions for periodic technical inspection
- It shall be possible to confirm the correct operational status of the AEBS by visible observation of the failure warning signal status after power-on.
- Protection against simple unauthorized modification of the failure warning signal operation shall be outlined.

## 6. Test procedure
### 6.1. Test conditions
- Flat, dry concrete or asphalt surface with good adhesion.
- Ambient temperature between 0 °C and 45 °C.
- Adequate horizontal visibility and no wind affecting results.

### 6.2. Vehicle conditions
- Test weight as agreed between manufacturer and Technical Service.

### 6.3. Test targets
- Target shall be a regular high volume series production passenger car of category M1 (AA saloon) or a representative soft target.

### 6.4. Warning and activation test with a stationary target
- Subject vehicle approaches stationary target at 80 ± 2 km/h.
- Warning timing and minimum speed reduction requirements are specified in Annex 3, Table I.
- Emergency braking phase shall not start before a TTC ≤ 3.0 seconds.

### 6.5. Warning and activation test with a moving target
- Subject vehicle (80 ± 2 km/h) approaches moving target (speed per Annex 3, Table I).
- Warning timing requirements are specified in Annex 3, Table I.
- Emergency braking shall result in no impact with the moving target.
- Emergency braking phase shall not start before a TTC ≤ 3.0 seconds.

### 6.6. Failure detection test
- Simulate an electrical failure. The failure warning signal shall activate within 10 seconds of driving above 15 km/h and remain activated.

### 6.7. Deactivation test
- For vehicles with deactivation means, verify warning signal activates upon deactivation and system reinstates after ignition cycle.

### 6.8. False reaction test
- Subject vehicle passes between two stationary vehicles at 50 ± 2 km/h. The AEBS shall not provide a collision warning or initiate emergency braking.

## 7. Modification of vehicle type and extension of approval
Modifications shall be notified to the Type Approval Authority, which may grant an extension or require further tests.

## 8. Conformity of production
Procedures shall conform to the general provisions of the Agreement. The Type Approval Authority may verify conformity.

## 9. Penalties for non-conformity of production
Approval may be withdrawn if conformity of production requirements are not met.

## 10. Production definitively discontinued
The approval holder shall inform the Type Approval Authority upon ceasing manufacture.

## 11. Names and addresses of Technical Services and Type Approval Authorities
Contracting Parties shall communicate relevant names and addresses to the UN Secretariat.

## 12. Transitional provisions
- From the entry into force of the 01 series of amendments, Contracting Parties applying it shall not refuse type approvals according to it.
- Until 1 November 2016, Contracting Parties shall not refuse type approval of vehicles approved to the 00 series.
- From 1 November 2016, Contracting Parties applying the 01 series are not obliged to accept vehicles approved to the 00 series for national/regional type approval.

## Annexes
- **Annex 1**: Communication form for approval notifications.
- **Annex 2**: Arrangements of approval marks.
- **Annex 3**: Warning and activation test requirements – Pass/fail values (Table I).
- **Annex 4**: Special requirements to be applied to the safety aspects of complex electronic vehicle control systems (definitions, documentation, safety concept, verification).
